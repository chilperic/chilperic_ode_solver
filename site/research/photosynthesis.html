Research programme · unpublished work

Mechanistic evolution across the C3–C4 continuum

I study how coupled biochemical, hydraulic, thermal, anatomical and resource constraints can shape the evolutionary accessibility of photosynthetic phenotypes. The public page describes the questions and scientific strategy; the complete model remains private while the work is unpublished.

The question I want the model to answer

Starting from plausible C3-like populations, which heritable changes can produce viable C3–C4 intermediate and C4-like phenotypes under different environmental histories, and which apparent routes disappear once physiological costs and uncertainty are taken seriously?

  • Which anatomical and transport changes become advantageous before, during or after recruitment of a carbon-concentrating mechanism?
  • How do temperature, drought, atmospheric demand, soil water and water-temperature-dependent hydraulics alter the selective landscape?
  • When are intermediate mechanisms genuinely beneficial rather than artifacts of parameter bounds or an incomplete cost model?
  • How sensitive are inferred routes to uncertain kinetics, anatomy, hydraulic vulnerability, resource allocation, inheritance and mutational covariance?
  • Can different climates generate multiple accessible routes rather than one universal C3→C4 sequence?

Scientific strategy

I separate three problems that are easy to conflate.

  1. Physiology: determine whether a phenotype satisfies carbon, water, heat, energetic and resource constraints.
  2. Landscape analysis: map performance, trade-offs, sensitivity and uncertainty without interpreting an optimizer path as evolutionary history.
  3. Evolution: simulate populations with heritable variation, reproduction, selection and drift, then ask which routes recur across replicate populations and environments.

C2 photosynthesis is not the organizing target. It is treated as one possible intermediate photorespiratory mechanism when the biology and the cost/benefit accounting require it. The central object is the full C3–C4 continuum.

What I consider scientifically non-negotiable

  • Assimilation is a physiological output, not reproductive fitness by itself.
  • CMA-ES, NSGA-II, grids and continuation are search or landscape tools, not biological generations.
  • Failed, nonphysical or unresolved simulations are failures, not low-fitness phenotypes.
  • Water-temperature effects on viscosity are separated from pits, conduit connectivity, outside-xylem resistance, aquaporins and vulnerability.
  • Parameters are tagged as measured, literature-anchored, calibrated, derived, assumed, scenario or missing.
  • Uncertain or missing quantities are not silently converted into precise biological constants.

Current public evidence

Earlier project figures include sensitivity analyses, Pareto analyses and numerical search traces under contrasting environmental scenarios. I retain them as research provenance, but I do not present optimizer traces as reconstructed evolutionary histories. Quantitative interpretation depends on the exact model version, forcing, parameter distributions, constraints and treatment of failed simulations.

Protected research boundary

The complete equations, executable model, raw parameter sets, calibration material, uncertainty priors, unpublished hypotheses, notebooks and source code are not distributed on this public site. The public repository contains only a non-reconstructive project description for this research programme.

This boundary is intentional: a browser-side password would not protect source files shipped to a public website. Unpublished scientific material must remain in private storage or an authenticated server-side research environment.
