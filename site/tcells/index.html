Model, equations & interpretation

Birth–death ODE

The linear birth–death family is reviewed in the AIMS thesis (2016), section 2.2. Here dN₀/dt = −(π₀+δ₀)N₀, and dNᵢ/dt = 2πᵢ₋₁Nᵢ₋₁ − (πᵢ+δᵢ)Nᵢ for i > 0, with πₕ = 0. Nᵢ is an expected count, so fractional values are meaningful. Exact Gillespie realizations and their ensemble moments accompany the ODE. There is no separate activation phase; α is unused.

Cyton · independent competing clocks

At birth, each cell is division-competent with probability fᵢ. A competent cell independently draws a division time and a death time; the earlier event occurs. A noncompetent cell only has a death clock. Daughters draw new independent clocks. Terminal cells have fₕ = 0. For exact ties in fixed lifetimes, death takes precedence.

If Fdiv and Fdeath are lifetime CDFs, cell survival is Sᵢ(t) = [1 − Fdeath(t)][1 − fᵢFdiv(t)]. The unconditional division-event kernel is fᵢ[1 − Fdeath(t)] dFdiv(t). These replace survival and the division kernel in the renewal equations below. The lifetime families can differ, with separate mean lifetimes; when a shape/CV setting applies to both families, it is shared. This minimal Cyton implementation has no inherited division destiny, correlated siblings, fluorescence observation model or data fitting.

Bellman–Harris and Kimmel–Axelrod frameworks

The AIMS thesis (2016), printed pp. 14–15, distinguishes continuous-age Bellman–Harris processes from discrete multitype Galton–Watson processes, citing Kimmel and Axelrod among the latter’s references. These are frameworks, not unique parameterized models. The two options here use explicit independent three-fate rules: division with probability pᵢ, permanent quiescence with probability qᵢ, and death with probability 1−pᵢ−qᵢ. Daughters draw their types independently. Quiescent cells remain alive for the whole experiment.

The Bellman–Harris option samples fate-specific continuous lifetimes. The Kimmel–Axelrod framework option uses synchronous one-cycle lifetimes; its time unit is a cycle, not an hour, and its reference moments use an exact discrete recurrence. It is an illustrative multitype Galton–Watson variant, not a claimed reproduction of a specific fitted Kimmel–Axelrod model.

Age-dependent renewal calculation

The age-dependent branching family is reviewed in the AIMS thesis (2016), section 2.2. Here, an explicit Bellman–Harris variant assigns each cell a fate at birth, then samples a lifetime conditional on division or death. Quiescent cells have infinite lifetime in the experiment. Division produces two generation-i+1 daughters; death produces none. This is a model extension, not a claimed reproduction of the thesis’s fitted models. Terminal cells have division probability zero.

Division and death lifetime families may be selected independently, with separate means. Shape and CV settings are shared when applicable to both families. Erlang shape controls the dispersion; k = 1 gives an exponential lifetime. Lognormal CV is standard deviation divided by mean. Fixed lifetimes synchronize cohorts. Changing the distribution while keeping means constant can change the population dynamics.

Reference moments follow renewal equations for one newborn ancestor: Mᵢ(t) = Sᵢ(t) + 2pᵢ ∫ Mᵢ₊₁(t−s) dFᵢ,div(s), and Rᵢ(t) = Sᵢ(t) + 2pᵢ ∫ [Rᵢ₊₁(t−s) + Mᵢ₊₁(t−s)²] dFᵢ,div(s). Sᵢ(t) = qᵢ + pᵢ[1−Fdiv(t)] + (1−pᵢ−qᵢ)[1−Fdeath(t)] is survival under the three-fate lifetime mixture; R is the raw second moment. Independent founders multiply the one-ancestor mean and variance. Generation-specific moments use the same recursion with survival contributing only to the ancestor’s own generation.

Renewal integrals start at 960 intervals, compared with 480, and refine up to 3,840 until the maximum normalized change in the displayed total mean and variance is at most 1%. The comparison reports when this criterion remains unmet. The displayed resolution difference is a convergence diagnostic, not a rigorous error bound. Sharp or fixed lifetimes can be sensitive to grid alignment. The event simulation itself schedules continuous lifetimes without a time grid. Reference comparisons for this variant must account for both sampling noise and numerical approximation.

Activation model · 2019

Dr. Chilperic Armel Foko Kuate, Mathematical models of T cell proliferation, with potential applications to data (2019), chapter 4, printed pages 28–34, especially equation (4.1.8). The implementation uses the reaction specification on page 31, with constant, generation-specific rates.

Qᵢ → Aᵢ   at hazard αᵢ Qᵢ

Aᵢ → 2 Qᵢ₊₁   at hazard πᵢ Aᵢ   (i < h)

Aᵢ → ∅   at hazard δᵢ Aᵢ

Qᵢ and Aᵢ count quiescent and activated cells that have divided i times. A division removes one parent and adds two daughters, so the living population rises by one. A death reduces it by one.

Rates are not probabilities

For a quiescent cell, waiting time to activation is exponential with mean 1/αᵢ. Once activated, division and death compete: the waiting time to either event has mean 1/(πᵢ + δᵢ), and the next event is division with probability πᵢ/(πᵢ + δᵢ). If both rates are zero, that cell remains activated indefinitely. At generation h, only death can follow activation.

Independent reference calculation

The stochastic solver uses the Gillespie direct method. A separate calculation integrates the exact closed first and second moment equations for the same linear reaction network:

m′ = Bm

C′ = BC + CBᵀ + Σᵣ νᵣνᵣᵀ aᵣ(m)

m is the vector of mean counts; C is their covariance matrix; νᵣ is the change caused by reaction r; aᵣ is its hazard. B is the linear mean-dynamics matrix. Total population variance sums every entry of C, including cross-generation covariances. Deterministic starting counts give C(0) = 0.

Moment equations use fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration with a rate-dependent step limit. Curves and the generation table use 121 equally spaced observation times. Playback follows exact event times for run 1; the generation table displays the preceding observation time. The plotted run-1 curve connects sampled counts.

Scope and limits

This is a numerical implementation of the specified model, with illustrative parameter presets. It does not fit CFSE data or simulate fluorescence, Myc regulation, cell interactions, fixed delays or general waiting-time distributions. Smith–Martin fixed-phase delays remain a separate, unimplemented model. Birth–death, age-dependent and Cyton options are specified above. No empirical validation is claimed.

Up to 50 founders, 8 divisions, 500 repetitions and 240 hours are supported. An experiment stops with an error if it exceeds 2,000,000 events; incomplete ensembles are not displayed. The lineage view shows one selectable founder from run 1. All lineages contribute to the population statistics. JSON exports include model provenance, settings, moments and the first two recorded cell histories; other runs are summarized.
