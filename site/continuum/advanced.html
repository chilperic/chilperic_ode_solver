Region boundaries, biological meaning & reference species

The colors classify the modeled enzyme budget, not plant species. The literature supports the physiological descriptions below; it does not establish these numerical cutoffs. Regions remain fixed when climate changes; performance and feasibility can change.

C3 region
Mesophyll Rubisco ≥90% of the total modeled enzyme budget. Biological context: direct Rubisco assimilation, photorespiration and electron-transport limitation in the C3 biochemical model of Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry.
C4 region
Mesophyll Rubisco ≤10%, with PEPC ≥10% and sheath Rubisco ≥10%. Biological context: a coupled PEPC carbon pump and bundle-sheath Rubisco assimilation. The 2021 model uses parameterization from Setaria viridis. Flaveria bidentis is also a C4 reference species (Baros, Beerkens & Ludwig, 2024); it is not an intermediate.
C3–C4 intermediate region
Each of the three allocation fractions ≥5%, after assigning C3 and C4 first. This is a mixed-allocation proxy. Biological intermediacy also involves photorespiratory CO2 recycling, glycine decarboxylase localization and variable C4-cycle contribution; these mechanisms cannot be diagnosed from allocation alone. Flaveria ramosissima is a literature example of an intermediate. Mallmann et al. investigate the C2 pump and associated nitrogen balance; Ku et al. compare photosynthetic and photorespiratory traits across Flaveria.
Other allocation
All remaining allocations. A state outside these illustrative classes is not automatically infeasible, and an allocation color does not guarantee viability. Hatching shows the separate feasibility evaluation. No measured species coordinates are plotted.

Equations, assumptions & interpretation

pfix=1−e−2s1−e−4Nes

At s = 0, pfix = 1/(2Ne). The implementation follows the diploid convention in your v13 document, equations 72–73. Transition rate Qij = 2N μ qij pfix(sij,Ne), where sij = exp(κ(rj−ri))−1. Exponential waiting times and rate-weighted destinations define a continuous-time substitution process.

N controls mutation supply; Ne controls fixation. The declared kernel is uniform over neighboring allocation/conductance grid states. Infeasible destinations consume mutation probability but cannot fix. The nearest initial grid state is shown in the exported record. Fixation duration, standing variation and recombination are omitted. The software requires 2N μ Ne ≤ 0.1 as a conservative screen; this is not a proof that origin–fixation assumptions hold.

Combination with optimization: the same forcing, equations, traits and performance weights feed the grid, CMA-ES, NSGA-II or epsilon comparator. Search results do not bias the mutation kernel. Pareto dominance uses carbon ↑, water ↓, heat ↓; biological fixation still needs its declared scalar fitness mapping.

Sobol: independent uniform Monte Carlo A/B matrices, Saltelli first-order and Jansen total-order estimates; 100 paired bootstrap resamples. Negative or >1 finite-sample estimates remain visible. Compare the half-sample estimate and increase sample size before trusting ranks. This computes Sobol indices; it does not use a Sobol low-discrepancy sequence.

Morris: randomized 4-level trajectories with Δ = 2/3. μ* screens magnitude; signed μ shows average direction; σ reflects nonlinearity and/or interactions. Effects are scaled to the declared input ranges. Rankings are conditional on the chosen target and ranges.

PCA: standardize two allocation log-ratios and gs. For boundary states use ε = 10⁻⁶ in log((fi+ε)/(fsheath+ε)), explicitly recorded. Kimura states are weighted equally per recorded event, not by residence time. Loadings and explained variance describe this dataset; they are not sensitivity indices or selection coefficients.

Methods references: SALib sensitivity documentation · Saltelli/Jansen estimators · PCA interpretation. These browser implementations are tested against analytical benchmarks; they are not calls to SALib or scikit-learn.

Season length and evolutionary time

A selection period can be one repeated day or a growing season of 1–365 days. Season blocks sample location, solar geometry and a dated stress window. Their durations sum to the requested period; choose 3, 6 or 9 blocks to assess sampling sensitivity.

Cseason=∑ikAiΔti,C¯=CseasonDseason

Carbon, water and heat totals integrate each block. The selection score uses mean daily performance so increasing season length alone does not multiply selection strength. Mutation waiting times and the evolutionary horizon remain in generations.

Soil wetness is a prescribed boundary in this leaf-physiology landscape. It is not replenished rainfall or a simulated root-zone water store. Use Plant growth for a finite soil-water balance. This selection model does not yet compute seed-yield fitness, evolving flowering dates, or climate change between generations.

Season timing is biologically motivated by work such as Franks, Sim & Weis (2007), drought and flowering-time evolution. That study is context, not calibration of these parameters.
