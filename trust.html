How to interpret a result

What Foko Lab computed — and where the claim stops.

Every capability carries a label that tells you whether the result was computed in the browser, produced by a bounded method, prepared for an external tool, or not offered.

User GuideTutorialsModel Atlas

Browser-computed

The visible result is computed locally from the current input in browser code.

Limited

A bounded or heuristic browser implementation is available; stronger claims require external validation.

Export-only

The platform generates a reproducible external workflow but does not claim to execute it in the browser.

Unavailable

Not presented as a computed capability.

32/32 representative comparisons passed against independently maintained numerical libraries. This is regression evidence for the tested cases, not certification of every model or conclusion.

Known limitations
  • Requested tolerances constrain the numerical solve; they do not quantify model-form, parameter, measurement or biological uncertainty.
  • Changing a scientific or numerical input marks existing evidence stale; stale plots are visible as prior evidence but result/image exports are disabled until rerun.
  • Initial conditions, parameter values and ranges, time span, output density, method, step controls, rtol, atol and safety are user-editable and validated before computation.
  • Browser methods are intended for moderate explicit systems.
  • The local Jacobian timescale ratio and rejection evidence are stiffness heuristics, not a stiffness certificate.
  • Verify against SciPy is an optional independent numerical comparison; first use requires network access unless a local Pyodide distribution is configured, and agreement does not validate the model or data.
  • The Model Report Card records computation and declared limits; it is not a scientific-validity certificate.
  • Publication-grade results require independent convergence and domain validation.
  • The foko.model-ir/1 reaction-network route is a limited local schema, not SBML, CellML, Modelica or a DAE/hybrid-system standard.
  • Reaction rates and direct equations still use the platform restricted expression evaluator and must be independently checked.
  • The fatty-acid and FADNS browser examples are reduced public research models, not the complete calibrated repositories; they require independent reproduction before publication use.

Known limitations
  • Nonlinear least squares is a local numerical search; convergence does not establish a global optimum or unique parameter vector.
  • Local covariance intervals and bands rely on a linearization of the fitted model and an appropriate residual error model.
  • Inverse-variance weighting treats the supplied sigma values as known, strictly positive observation standard deviations.
  • Pairs bootstrap resamples rows as exchangeable units and is not valid for clustered, serially dependent or hierarchical observations without redesign.
  • Profile SSE scans, local correlation matrices and the practical-identifiability verdict are finite local diagnostics; they do not certify structural identifiability or global uniqueness.
  • Experimental-design advice is a local covariance-based sensitivity heuristic over a finite candidate grid; it does not guarantee identifiability or an optimal experiment.
  • In-sample R-squared, AIC, residual plots and influence measures do not establish out-of-sample validity or mechanistic truth.

Known limitations
  • The browser reference is intended for small dense matrices and uses floating-point arithmetic.
  • Singular values are estimated from eigenvalues of A-transpose A; very small singular values are sensitive to squaring the condition number.
  • Rank and nullity depend on the selected numerical tolerance and matrix scaling.
  • The browser eigensolver accepts real symmetric matrices only; general nonsymmetric spectra require an external library.
  • A small residual does not imply a well-conditioned or scientifically correct system.
  • PCA is centered but not standardized and remains sensitive to scale and outliers.
  • A stationary Markov candidate does not certify uniqueness, irreducibility or mixing time.

Known limitations
  • Nested and repeated cross-validation reduce optimistic selection bias but do not create external validity; small samples can still produce highly variable estimates.
  • Cross-validation estimates internal performance for the supplied sample and does not establish external validity or robustness to distribution shift.

  • Large boosted ensembles, deep learning, nested tuning, GPU training and production deployment require external workflows.
  • PCA shown beside supervised or clustering results is computed from the same prepared feature matrix and remains descriptive rather than predictive validation.

Known limitations
  • Exact-count mode requires non-negative integer state counts summing exactly to N². Fraction mode uses deterministic largest-remainder allocation and reports the exact counts actually simulated.
  • Custom Agent models are restricted to validated declarative local transition rules (spontaneous, neighbour-contact and neighbour-threshold). Arbitrary JavaScript is not executed.
  • One algorithmic step is one configured lattice sweep. Random-with-replacement gives one update attempt per site on average; shuffled sweep visits every site once. Neither is automatically biological, social, epidemiological or physical time.
  • Finite-ensemble quantiles and Monte Carlo standard errors describe simulation variability under fixed rules and parameters; they do not quantify parameter uncertainty, measurement error or model discrepancy.
  • Cluster counts, largest-cluster fractions, spatial agreement and entropy are descriptive lattice diagnostics whose interpretation depends on state coding, occupancy, topology, initialization and boundary conditions.
  • Finite-horizon terminal labels and absorption times are conditional on the configured grid, horizon, update schedule and seed. They are not stationary-distribution or asymptotic certificates.
  • The curated T-cell, cell-cycle, fatty-acid, SIR, voter, segregation, predator-prey and forest-fire examples are qualitative mechanisms or stress tests rather than calibrated empirical models.
  • Continuous-space, network-agent, adaptive, reinforcement-learning and large-scale agent simulations require external workflows.
  • Worker cancellation terminates the active ensemble and publishes no partial result. A main-thread fallback is used only when Web Workers are unavailable and is explicitly disclosed.
  • When Plotly cannot mount, the interface renders the same computed result through a built-in canvas fallback and labels the rendering path.
  • The fatty-acid particle presets are research-inspired lattice abstractions, not the published semi-mechanistic FADNS ODE and not quantitative kinetic models.
  • Dynamic lattice snapshots come from the representative derived seed; ensemble bands and endpoint distributions summarize independent runs and must not be conflated.
  • State legends are placed below categorical lattice panels to preserve the data region.
  • During computation, both visible Agent panels are driven by paced incremental numerical states from the same representative seeded realization. The pacing delay changes only display timing, not the seeded result; stored-frame replay is manual and non-looping.

Known limitations
  • All results are conditional on the selected output, initial conditions, time window, parameter ranges, perturbation or sample count, solver and tolerances.
  • Local finite differences are local and step-dependent; discontinuities, event thresholds and poor scaling can invalidate derivative interpretations.
  • Initial conditions affect trajectories but are not treated as sensitivity factors in this release.
  • Morris is a screening method. Its sigma combines interaction and nonlinearity and does not identify either mechanism separately.
  • The Jansen implementation assumes independent uniform input ranges. Raw finite-sample estimates may be negative or greater than one and are intentionally not clipped.
  • The Fisher-information view is a local range-scaled trajectory approximation under a constant independent noise scale; normalized column alignment is not posterior parameter correlation.
  • Browser-scale sample budgets are diagnostic rather than publication-grade. Larger studies require independent SALib/SciPy or equivalent workflows with convergence checks.
  • No adjoint, sensitivity-equation, eFAST, Shapley, correlated-input, symbolic-identifiability, profile-likelihood, certified continuation or PDE/PINN sensitivity engine is present.

Hard limits that remain

  • The browser is not a substitute for production stiff integration, large sparse algebra, large graph infrastructure, GPU training, structural-identifiability proof, causal inference, or empirical calibration.
  • Optional SciPy verification is a numerical referee and may require a network-loaded Pyodide runtime on first use.
  • Share URLs preserve configuration, not old computed evidence. Recipients must rerun.
  • Representative differential checks are regression evidence, not universal certification.
