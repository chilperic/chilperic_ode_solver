Research programme · unpublished work

Mechanistic evolution across the C3–C4 continuum

I study how biochemical, hydraulic, thermal, anatomical and resource constraints shape the evolutionary accessibility of photosynthetic phenotypes. This public page describes the scientific questions and strategy without publishing the full model.

Central question

Starting from plausible C3-like populations, which heritable changes can produce viable C3–C4 intermediate and C4-like phenotypes under different environmental histories, and which apparent routes disappear once physiological costs and uncertainty are represented?

  • Which anatomical and transport changes become advantageous as carbon-concentrating mechanisms are recruited?
  • How do temperature, drought, atmospheric demand, soil water and water-temperature-dependent hydraulics alter the landscape?
  • When are intermediate mechanisms genuinely beneficial rather than consequences of incomplete costs or restrictive parameter bounds?
  • How sensitive are inferred routes to uncertain kinetics, anatomy, hydraulic vulnerability, resource allocation, inheritance and mutational covariance?
  • Can contrasting climates generate multiple accessible routes rather than one universal sequence?

Scientific strategy

  1. Physiology: test carbon, water, heat, energetic and resource feasibility.
  2. Landscape analysis: map performance, trade-offs, sensitivity and uncertainty without treating an optimizer path as evolutionary history.
  3. Evolution: simulate populations with heritable variation, reproduction, selection and drift and compare replicated trajectories across environments.

The project examines the C3–C4 continuum, including photorespiratory intermediates where relevant to the biological hypothesis.

Scientific rules

  • Assimilation is a physiological output, not reproductive fitness by itself.
  • Optimization and biological evolution are kept separate.
  • Failed or nonphysical simulations are recorded as failures.
  • Water-temperature effects on viscosity are separated from biological hydraulic resistances and vulnerability.
  • Parameters are identified as measured, literature-anchored, calibrated, derived, assumed, scenario or missing.

Research availability

This research is ongoing. Selected figures and interactive simulations are available; the full model and calibration data are not yet released.
