Mechanistic evolution across the C3–C4 continuum

The research goal is not to force plants through predefined categories. It is to test which heritable physiological and anatomical changes can arise, persist and combine under explicit environmental and resource constraints.

Primary scientific questions

  • Starting from plausible C3-like populations, which trait changes are physiologically viable and evolutionarily accessible?
  • Which combinations of anatomy, carbon-concentrating mechanisms, hydraulics, thermal regulation and resource allocation create reproducible paths toward C3–C4 intermediate or C4-like phenotypes?
  • How do climate history, drought, temperature, water temperature, uncertainty, mutation, inheritance, selection and drift change those paths?
  • Which apparent optima disappear when energetic, protein/nitrogen, hydraulic and structural costs are represented?

Scientific separation

Physiology determines whether a phenotype is physically and biologically admissible. Landscape methods such as parameter sweeps or numerical optimization identify high-performing regions. Evolution requires a population model with heritable variation, reproduction, selection and drift. Optimizer iterations are not presented as biological generations.

C2 is not a central target. It is one possible photorespiratory intermediate mechanism and is included only where it is required by the biological hypothesis and has an explicit effect on the modeled costs and benefits.

Public/private boundary

The complete equations, executable core, raw parameter sets, calibration data, uncertainty priors and unpublished mechanistic hypotheses are not public. The website exposes the research questions, model classes, limitations, selected non-reconstructive figures and attribution only.

Parameter evidence

FokoLab distinguishes measured, literature anchor, calibrated, derived, assumed, scenario and missing quantities. A missing or uncertain parameter is not presented as a measured biological constant.
