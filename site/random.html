Distribution definitions & sources

Continuous increments are independent. μ and σ are the mean and standard deviation for Normal, Laplace and the symmetric triangular law. Laplace scale is σ/√2; triangular support is μ ± √6σ.

Exponential uses rate λ. Gamma uses shape k and scale θ. Lognormal uses the mean and standard deviation of log(X). Beta uses shapes α and β on [0, 1]. Weibull uses shape k and scale λ. Student t uses location μ, scale σ and ν degrees of freedom; σ is not its standard deviation. At ν ≤ 2 its variance does not exist, and at ν = 1 its mean does not exist.

Positive laws are summed as positive increments; a cumulative sum is not automatically a renewal counting process. The mean-zero comparison for these laws uses a separately labeled Gaussian reference.

Definitions: NumPy Gamma, Student t, SciPy Beta and Lognormal. These are mathematical definitions, not empirical calibration data.

What these models mean

Random walks accept discrete custom increments, normal increments uniform, Laplace or symmetric triangular increments. For the new laws μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation: Laplace scale b = σ/√2; triangular support [μ − √6σ, μ + √6σ]. Brownian motion uses independent Gaussian increments with variance σ² Δt; the drawn lines interpolate sampled positions, not the exact continuous path.

Galton–Watson: every individual samples an independent offspring count each generation. Poisson mean = λ; binomial mean = Kp; geometric mean = (1 − p)/p. No persistence is applied to offspring.

Gillespie is an exact event-sampling algorithm for the specified birth–death–immigration model: wait an exponential time with rate λN + μN + ν, then choose the event proportional to its hazard. The animation reveals event order per run; horizontal coordinates preserve actual event times. It stops at the time horizon or event cap. Zero is absorbing when immigration is zero.

Population simulations stop before recording a population above 1,000,000; unfinished runs are reported. Any custom weights must be finite and nonnegative, and at least one must be positive.

These are finite mathematical experiments, not validated biological models. A branching picture alone does not establish fractality.

The tree counts visits by outcome prefix. Colony counts merge directed segments with matching endpoints (rounded to six decimals). Chaos-game points show individual arrivals, not a density estimate.

Long tree paths taper to 5% of their starting length; compact branches shrink to 32% each step. Paths may cross without sharing a branch.

For colonies, every decision costs 0.2 energy and each attempted extension costs another 0.8. Barriers terminate colliding tips. Each resource patch has 25 energy per colony, supplies at most 2 per arrival, and attracts tips while nonempty. Rings mark fixed patch locations, not current remaining energy.

All models stop at 200,000 sampled decisions per panel. For Galton–Watson, each individual offspring draw counts toward this limit. Colonies may stop earlier when no growing tips remain. The default identical control copies every rule and the seed, so both panels grow identically. Alternative baselines deliberately change the selected rules; growth, extinction and stopping times can then differ. Both panels always share n, m and the same safety limits. Branching changes can consume random numbers in different orders.

In the triangle, faces 1–2, 3–4 and 5–6 select the three vertices. In the hexagon, each face selects one vertex. Initial transient points are included.
