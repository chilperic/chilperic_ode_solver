De novo synthesis · chapter 5

Source: Dr. Chilperic Armel Foko Kuate, Dynamic computational modeling of fatty acid de novo synthesis in the liver and qualitative analysis of fatty acid metabolism, PhD thesis, equations 5.1.3–5.1.28 and tables 5.4, 5.7. The preset uses the rounded reported fit parameters: β = 0.043, κ = 0.12, δ = 1.3 × 10⁻⁶, kd6 = 1.16, kd7 = 0.03, kd8 = 0.7, kfCoA = 0.041 and kbCoA = 0.00026, with recalculated substrate concentrations and 0.153 μM total FAS.

E + Acetyl-CoA → EC2 + CoA; v = β E Acetyl-CoA

EC2i + Malonyl-CoA + 2 NADPH → EC2i+2 + CoA; v = κ EC2i Malonyl-CoA NADPH

EC16 → EC18 uses δ instead of κ.

EC14/16/18 → E + C14:0/16:0/18:0; v = kd × EC

E + CoA ⇌ ECoA; v = kf E CoA − kb ECoA

These are reduced reaction equations: water, NADP⁺ and released CO₂ are not explicit states. The consumption coefficient of NADPH is two, although its exponent in the empirical elongation rate law is one. The no-inhibition comparison changes only reversible CoA binding, retaining all other rates and initial conditions. It does not refit the model.

Conserved quantities checked here are total enzyme; the modeled CoA moieties; NADPH − 2 malonyl-CoA; and retained acyl-carbon equivalents. The last assigns two retained carbons per malonyl unit, so it is not a complete elemental carbon balance. Initial enzyme is free; initial enzyme complexes and fatty-acid products are zero. Concentrations use μM and time uses seconds. No experimental observations have been imported, and the original fit has not been reproduced.

Four-pool metabolism · chapter 4

A′ = k1 − r1 + r4 − αA

M′ = r1 − r2

F′ = k2 + r2 − r3 − r4 + r5 − βF

T′ = k3 + r3 − r5 − γT

r1 = V1 A / [(K1+A)(1+q1 F)]

r2 = V2 M/(K2+M); r3 = V3 F/(K3+F)

r4 = V4 F / [(K4+F)(1+q4 M)]

r5 = V5 T/(K5+T)

A, M, F and T denote acetyl-CoA, malonyl-CoA, fatty-acid and triglyceride pools. These are the rate laws in table 4.1 and equations 4.1.5–4.1.8. Parameters are illustrative, with arbitrary pool and time units. The lumped unit stoichiometry is preserved; these pools must not be interpreted as an elemental carbon balance. The comparison starts all four pools at 10 times the entered initial values, with identical rates.

Why the equilibrium result needs review

At steady state, T is the unique nonnegative solution of γT + V5 T/(K5+T) = k3 + V3 F/(K3+F), so T increases with F. Summing the four balance equations gives A = (k1+k2+k3 − βF − γT)/α, which strictly decreases with F when α, β and γ are positive.

Consequently r1 decreases with F. Where r1 < V2, the condition r1 = r2 gives M = K2 r1/(V2−r1), also decreasing with F. Therefore r4 increases with F, and H(F) = k1 − r1 + r4 − αA strictly increases on the admissible domain A ≥ 0, M ≥ 0. H can have at most one zero. Polynomial roots outside this domain cannot establish physical bistability.

This is an implementation-time mathematical deduction from the printed equations, not a claim that the thesis has been independently peer reviewed. The module does not alter those equations to produce bistability. It finds a physical zero of H by bisection and checks all four ODE residuals. Local stability uses the analytical Jacobian and the Routh criterion for its fourth-degree characteristic polynomial; near-degenerate cases are reported as unresolved. Uniqueness alone does not establish stability or convergence.

Numerical method and display

Both models use adaptive implicit Euler with step doubling and Newton solves. Negative states trigger step rejection, not clipping. The accepted result is the two-half-step solution. A hard iteration limit produces an error rather than partial results. The method supports stiff enzyme kinetics; its local tolerance is not a global error guarantee.

Animation interpolates 201 stored states. Arrows indicate current rates; moving markers are flow cues, not individual molecules. Enzyme occupancy bars show fractions of the total enzyme. Charts display the complete calculated trajectory while the cursor follows playback. Reset restores the preset; edited values are included in exports.
