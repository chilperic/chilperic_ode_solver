Temperature, water and evaporation

These equations are visible without running an experiment. Change humidity, water conductance or soil temperature, then inspect the temperature, VPD, transpiration and evaporation plots.

1. Air humidity and leaf-to-air vapour pressure deficit

Dleaf=max(0,esat(Tleaf)−RHesat(Tair))

Dleaf = max(0, esat(Tleaf) − RH × esat(Tair)). RH is a fraction, so 65% is 0.65. Air humidity refers to air temperature, while saturated vapour pressure inside the leaf refers to leaf temperature. Dew is excluded by the maximum with zero.

2. Saturated vapour pressure

esat(T)=610.8exp(17.27TT+237.3)

esat(T) = 610.8 exp[17.27 T/(T + 237.3)] Pa, with T in °C. The heat solver uses kelvin internally and converts for this approximation.

3. Stomata and boundary layer act in series

gtw=11gsw+1gbw

gtw = (1/gsw + 1/gbw)⁻¹. gsw is stomatal conductance to water; gbw is boundary-layer conductance to water. Units: mol m⁻² leaf s⁻¹. gm in the carbon model is mesophyll CO₂ conductance and is not substituted into this evaporation equation.

4. Leaf transpiration and evaporative cooling

E=gtwDleafP

E = gtw Dleaf/P, mol H₂O m⁻² leaf s⁻¹. D and atmospheric pressure P must use the same units. Latent heat loss is λE; growth uses λ = 44,000 J/mol and P = 101,325 Pa. The research adapter exposes its pressure and uses its source λ. At fixed temperature/conductance, higher RH reduces E; the coupled leaf temperature can change in response.

5. Exposed soil evaporation

Esoil=gsoilw2max(0,esat(Tsurface)−RHesat(Tair))P

Esoil = gsoil w² max[0, esat(Tsurface) − RH esat(Tair)]/P, mol m⁻² ground s⁻¹. w is relative bucket wetness. This conductance law is illustrative, not a fitted soil evaporation model. Growth limits E and Esoil jointly by available water; transpiration has priority.

6. Solved leaf temperature

0=Qabsorbed−Hair−λE−Rsky−Rsoil−Qxylem−Qloop

Absorbed shortwave − convection − latent cooling − net longwave to sky and soil − sensible heat gained by xylem water − optional circulation heat = 0, W m⁻² leaf. H = h(Tleaf − Tair); growth uses h = 20 + 15√wind W m⁻² K⁻¹. Temperature is a solved state, not an imposed offset. Leaves are quasi-steady; no leaf heat capacity is included.

7. Soil surface heat storage

CsurfacedTsurfacedt=Qsoil+LAIRleaf,soil−Rsoil,sky−Hsoil−λEsoil−Gsurface,root−Qwater,surface

Surface heat storage = absorbed shortwave + leaf longwave input − longwave to sky − convection − evaporation − conduction to roots − water-redistribution sensible heat. Soil fluxes use W m⁻² ground. Soil convective conductance is 8 + 4√wind in growth and 12 W m⁻² K⁻¹ in the research adapter.

8. Root-zone heat storage

CrootdTrootdt=Gsurface,root−Groot,deep+Qwater,root+LAIQloop

Root-zone storage = conductive input from surface − conduction to deep soil + sensible heat from water redistribution + LAI × optional circulation heat. Gsurface,root = k(Tsurface − Troot)/[(dsurface + droot)/2]. Groot,deep = k(Troot − Tdeep)/(droot/2 + 0.5 m). The deep temperature is a fixed boundary, not another solved layer.

9. Heat carried by transpiration water

Qxylem=75.2E(Tleaf / sheath−Troot)

Qxylem = 75.2 E(Tleaf/sheath − Troot). Water heat capacity is approximated as 75.2 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Positive cools the leaf; negative warms it. The flow equals actual transpiration. This once-through flux remains distinct from the additional closed circulation hypothesis. It is not returned to the root zone.

10. Soil water balance

ΔWsoil=Wrain+Wirrigation−Woverflow−Wtranspiration−Wevaporation

W is mm over the fixed 0.25 m² ground footprint. For Δt hours: transpiration removes 64.8 E × LAI × Δt mm, and soil evaporation removes 64.8 Esoil × Δt mm. The latent cooling and water loss use the same flux, debited once. The research steady-state adapter instead prescribes wetness and checks E ≤ Kl(ψsoil − ψleaf,limit).

11. Growth-module stomatal response

gsw=g0+gmaxopenswater1+Dleaf,kPa2

Before hydraulic restriction: gsw = (0.002 + gmax × open × swater)/(1 + Dleaf,kPa/2). gmax is 0.22 for C3/C4 and 0.16 for CAM; these are illustrative constants. swater = clamp[water/(0.4 capacity), 0, 1]. C3/C4 opening = mean leaf PAR/(mean leaf PAR + 150); CAM opens at night. Water supply can reduce gsw further. The research adapter instead lets you enter or optimize gsw directly.

12. Root water-supply constraint

Wallowed=min(Wrequested,Wroot supply,Wavailable)

Growth limits requested transpiration by 0.05 × root dry mass × relative wetness L/h, and by the water available over the step. The coefficient is illustrative. Conductance is reduced to deliver that allowed flux while retaining the boundary-layer resistance. Soil evaporation then uses remaining water. These constraints are distinct from the research adapter’s water-potential inequality.

13. Optional closed circulation radiator

Qloop=I75.2Floopε(Tleaf / sheath−Troot)

I is zero for C3/CAM and for C4 when disabled or when its no-circulation leaf temperature meets the target. For eligible C4, Floop is selected by a bounded search from zero to the user maximum, after a transpiration-only heat solve. The flow stops if soil cannot receive heat. Floop is actual circulating water in mol m⁻² leaf s⁻¹; ε is an effective heat-exchange fraction. Qloop is W m⁻² leaf. The leaf loses Qloop and root soil gains LAI × Qloop, so internal transfer cancels in the whole-system heat balance. Circulation consumes no water; it may indirectly alter transpiration by changing temperature. Warmer root soil disables supplementary circulation. The maximum flow and temperature target are hypothesis parameters, not measured phloem regulation. The closed path is soil exchange → roots → xylem stem → leaf → phloem stem → roots → soil exchange; upward flow equals transpiration plus circulation, downward flow equals circulation. Thus net soil loss equals transpiration. Vascular pressures, transit times and internal plant water storage are unresolved. No pump-energy or anatomical feasibility constraint is included. Prescribed soil temperatures imply an external thermal bath.

14. Root length density

RLDcm/cm³=0.0001Mroot,drySRLm/gAgroundzrooted

RLD = 10⁻⁴ × root dry mass × specific root length / (ground area × rooted depth), in cm root per cm³ soil. Vein length density is mm vein / mm² leaf. Total vein length in metres = 1000 × density × leaf area in m². These are geometric descriptors, not universal hydraulic conductances.

15. Development clock and phyllochron

Δτthermal=max(0,min(Tdriver,Tceiling)−Tbase)Δthours24

Thermal time is °C days. Temperature driver is root-zone temperature before emergence and air temperature afterward. Wetness scales the pre-emergence increment. Divide accumulated time by the selected pathway multiplier for stage and leaf thresholds. Emergence, flowering, filling and maturity thresholds are editable; appearance is gated by a constant phyllochron, final leaf ceiling and flowering. Resource availability controls actual expansion. This does not reproduce photoperiod or vernalization responses.

16. Conservative stem mobilization

ΔCmobilized=Cstem(1−exp(−kmobilizeΔthours24))

During thermal-time filling, transfer ΔC from stem to reserve; recover ΔC / carbon fraction × tissue nitrogen fraction to the N reserve. Total C and N are unchanged by transfer. Later construction pays growth respiration and draws N. Leaf construction beyond available appearance capacity is redirected to stem. This is a coarse turnover hypothesis, not an explicit stem nonstructural-carbohydrate or phloem transport model.

Closures, units and numerical solution

Longwave leaf-to-soil transfer is εpair σ F(Tleaf⁴ − Tsurface⁴), where εpair = (1/εleaf + 1/εsoil − 1)⁻¹, soil emissivity is 0.96, and all radiative temperatures are kelvin. F = min(user fraction, 1/LAI) enforces reciprocal area exchange; soil receives LAI times the leaf-area flux. Leaf sky fraction is 1 − F; soil sky fraction is 1 − LAI F. Effective sky temperature = air temperature − the editable sky offset. This is aggregate exchange geometry, not resolved canopy radiation.

Ci = [cdry + 4.18 × 10⁶ θ] di J m⁻² K⁻¹, where θ = bucket water (m)/total modeled soil thickness. k = kdry + (kwet − kdry)w. Dry heat capacity and this linear conductivity interpolation are assumptions. The bucket tracks available water only; residual bound water, freezing, snow and dew are not represented. Subzero results are an unfrozen extrapolation.

The uniform-moisture bucket redistributes water between layers. With fs = dsurface/(dsurface + droot), Qwater,surface = 75.2 Esoil(1 − fs)(Tsurface − Troot), and Qwater,root = 75.2 LAI E fs(Tsurface − Troot). Accepted rain/irrigation mixes at air temperature; overflow bypasses mixing. Water temperatures and infiltration fronts are not resolved.

Growth uses backward Euler at 10-minute steps for soil storage and a coupled nonlinear solve. Canopy interception = 1 − exp(−0.6 LAI); mean received PAR = intercepted PAR/LAI, absorbed leaf shortwave = 0.65 × intercepted solar/LAI. These optical coefficients are illustrative. Leaf temperature drives photosynthesis, shoot maintenance and growth mobilization; root temperature drives root maintenance. Hydraulic conductance and N uptake have no additional calibrated root-temperature response.

The research model retains its two optical compartments and source optical coefficients, divides effective external longwave area equally, and adds conduction UA(Tmes − Tbs). Mesophyll loses latent heat; the sheath exchanges sensible heat with incoming xylem water. Its four temperatures are either solved at equilibrium, or surface/root temperatures are held by external baths. Its Jmax remains temperature-independent. Chemical energy stored in biomass and organ heat storage are omitted in both thermal approximations.

Source, corrections & scientific scope

Updated formulation: converts the source soil table’s cm H₂O to Pa (1 cm H₂O = 98.0665 Pa), evaluates ambient vapour pressure at air temperature, and includes boundary-layer resistance. Carbon chemistry uses µbar consistently, the O₂/Ko competitive term, and separate CO₂/water conductance ratios. A numerical compartment balance replaces the original bundle-sheath quadratic and its positive floor. Light-driven electron flow supplies ATP and NADPH; net dark exchange is respiration.

Energy assumptions: the whole-leaf photon budget is derived from the existing two-layer absorbed shortwave using 45% PAR and 4.57 µmol/J. Jmax is a linear-flow capacity held fixed during the temperature response. The PEPC pump uses two ATP per turnover and a selectable supply ceiling. Remaining ATP and NADPH are partitioned between mesophyll and sheath in proportion to their assigned Rubisco fractions. This transfer approximation does not resolve malate/3-PGA shuttles or cell-specific redox. Sheath O₂ is held at atmospheric partial pressure. The original common enzyme-capacity budget is not a calibrated nitrogen/protein budget.

Thermal limits: steady compartment temperatures, prescribed heat conductance, uniform atmospheric longwave temperature and the source’s simplified optical layers remain. Heat storage, separate sky/soil view factors, chemical-energy storage in the heat balance, hydraulic vulnerability, capacitance and cavitation are not represented.

Original-comparison details follow; its uncorrected soil-unit and biochemical limitations remain deliberately visible for comparison.

Explicit corrections to the source pathway

  • The environmental presets store b_air near 280–300 K. Preset air temperature is interpreted as b_air − 273.15 + a_air × irradiance, then bounded to the supported 5–45 °C range. The solver accepts an independent Celsius air-temperature input.
  • Biochemical rate outputs already use μmol m⁻² s⁻¹ under the source conversions. The browser does not apply ModelBuilder’s extra factor of 10⁶ to total assimilation.
  • Negative mesophyll net assimilation is retained in outputs and the objective, so displayed carbon balances use the same rate as the diffusion constraint. Enzyme capacities use the actual fractions directly; no unused-budget redistribution is reported.

Other source choices remain visible limitations: bundle-sheath assimilation has a 10⁻⁹ floor, negative quadratic discriminants are clipped as in the source, respiration is not scaled by allocation, gas-conductance conversions use 298.15 K, and there is no explicit electron-transport or ATP budget. Activations of the floor or discriminant safeguard are reported.

What the solver does

For the entered stomatal conductance, Newton iteration solves the two heat balances. Bisection then solves mesophyll CO₂ from diffusion = net assimilation. Transpiration must not exceed the soil-to-leaf hydraulic supply. Infeasible points produce a reason, not zero productivity.

The optional search evaluates a grid of feasible enzyme fractions and conductances and refines it locally. Conductance is bounded between 0.0001 and 3 mol m⁻² s⁻¹. It is a bounded numerical search, not CasADi/Ipopt, CMA-ES or a guarantee of the global optimum. The fixed-allocation calculations are checked against the uploaded CasADi equations with explicit input-unit corrections.

Boundaries between models

This is a steady-state leaf model. It has no CAM storage cycle, whole-plant biomass allocation, fertilizer response, dynamic soil water, evolutionary population or seasonal forcing. Those concepts must not be inferred from its optimized enzyme fractions. The separate plant-growth module supplies illustrative organ growth and location-linked environmental scenarios; it is not silently substituted for this research adapter.
