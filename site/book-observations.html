Observation models change what can be inferred

Source excerpt. Paragraphs and equations below retain the author’s wording and mathematical content. Browser typography is adapted; the complete original remains available.

Suppose a biochemical state x(t) is observed through

yi=ax(ti)+b+ϵi,ϵi∼N(0,σ2).

If a is unknown, the absolute scale of x can become confounded with parameters controlling amplitude. Adding an observation model is not bookkeeping; it can change the information geometry of the inverse problem.

Worked analysis: fluorescence confounding

For x(t)=x0e−kt and observations y=ax(t)+b, time-course shape identifies k well under adequate sampling, but a and x0 only appear as the product ax0 if neither is otherwise constrained. An optimizer can return unique numbers because of bounds or initialization, while the likelihood contains a ridge. The correct response is to reparameterize the identifiable product, add calibration information, or report non-identifiability.
