How plant form and organ counts are represented

Maize can develop around 18 leaves and may carry more than one harvestable ear. Four visible leaves describe an early stage, not a mature ceiling. An ear contains many kernels; each kernel is a botanical fruit.

Wheat and sorghum can have multiple shoots (tillers), sunflower forms can branch, agave leaves form a rosette, and prickly-pear pads are flattened stems. The 2D view places reproductive structures on the appropriate stems, branches or pads. The number of sites is adjustable because this growth model does not predict reproductive-organ initiation or survival.

Leaf and pad counts are computational tissue units, not measured emerged, collared or living-leaf counts. Calendar mode fills units from available resources; thermal mode also uses accumulated warmth and a final-leaf limit. The warm, supplied example runs for 90 days with thermal development; its timing remains illustrative.

How to read this plot

Drought resilience & target search

Compare the last completed run with drought and drought + heat. Drought removes rain and irrigation during the chosen window. Heat raises temperature at approximately constant air vapour pressure, increasing VPD. A recovery pulse restores water; other original conditions resume. This is a controlled stress test, not a climate-change projection.

Production matters: every feasible candidate must meet both absolute harvest-tissue thresholds and the selected recovery threshold. Harvest tissue is a dry-mass proxy, not predicted edible yield, viable seed number or fitness. Choose the organ appropriate to your crop and measured yield targets; the displayed defaults are illustrative. Grain filling, harvest index and viable seed production require further model development.

Productivity requirements prevent low growth from being rewarded simply for conserving water. Recovery is carbon uptake during the third day after drought, relative to the control over the same hours. Tissue loss is the sum of hourly decreases in structural dry mass; the model has no death or survival probability.

Search candidate physiological traits

Vary one or two existing model parameters. All candidates face identical climate histories and management. Parameter changes are hypotheses about traits, not validated genetic modifications. Enzyme-investment costs and genetic feasibility are not modeled; raising a capacity may improve the score simply because its biological cost is absent.

Where did the assimilated carbon go?

Carbon, water and nitrogen balances are checked for every completed run.

Carbon concentration & water-use efficiency

C3 fixes carbon after diffusion into the leaf. C4 solves mesophyll and bundle-sheath CO₂ from PEPC pumping, Rubisco fixation, sheath leakage and diffusion. In energy-budget mode, pumping and fixation share the available ATP and NADPH. CAM takes up CO₂ at night and releases stored carbon by day. These mechanisms remain uncalibrated for the illustrated species.

Uptake excludes whole-plant maintenance; these are uptake-based efficiencies, not measured net gas-exchange WUE. Intrinsic WUE = uptake / stomatal conductance (µmol CO₂/mol H₂O conductance). Cumulative carbon WUE = acquired g C / litres transpired. Growth WUE = gross new dry tissue / litres transpired; it includes reserve-supported growth, so it is not net biomass gain. Instantaneous WUE = uptake / transpiration. Carbon per received photon is a cumulative operating ratio, not an absorbed-light quantum yield. Ratios show “—” before meaningful water use. C4 pump and leakage are balanced realized rates; a negative leakage value means inward diffusion into the sheath. CAM daytime internal CO₂ remains prescribed, not solved.
